The benchmark

Luxury houses speak through images they control, and have judged them by eye. Focal reads the pictures a house shows with AI vision and puts numbers on what the eye sees: how distinct the house is, how far and which way it moves, whether it still looks like itself, and who made the pictures. It measures the industry's visual language; whether that language moves the business is a question it tests openly.

A collection leaves the runway and passes through other hands: the shop window, the campaign, the photographers who also shoot its rivals, the advertising. By the season's end it may no longer be the collection that was shown. Focal describes each stage in the same terms, sets it against the same stage at the other twenty-seven houses, and says what carried and whether it stood apart.

Season benchmark

What carried, and what stayed distinct?

The runway translated into numbers and followed into the shop window, the campaigns and the advertising, then set against the attention that followed.

  • TransmissionWhether the advertising moves the way the runway moved, season to season. Advertising can sell more plainly than the show and still follow its direction.
  • DistinctnessHow far the brand stands from its peers at each stage, the runway among runways and the advertising among advertising, and what sets it apart: the answers on which its pictures differ most from theirs.
  • MixHow much of each stage is campaign imagery, product on a model and product alone.
  • MovementHow far each stage has moved from the brand's own previous seasons. The advertising's movement is shown without a score, since staying recognisable is part of its job.

Fourteen of the twenty-eight have new directors since 2025. The reading is the same for each: transmission, distinctness, mix, movement. Not a sales forecast. A reading of what held.

Every picture a house shows is read four ways: its clothes, how it is made, its place on the axes of luxury, and its colour and light. The answers say what sets a house apart. The clothes come first.

Read as luxury

Most luxury pictures share a register: polished, posed and quiet. Questions asked of one picture at a time say little about what sets one apart from another, so each picture is also read against others like it. Five axes were tried; the two that passed the tests set before reading, remote to intimate and restrained to provocative, are the two read.

  1. Austeretoopulent

    Austere. Spare and reduced; few elements, plain surfaces, a quiet palette, nothing decorative.

    Opulent. Rich and abundant; ornament, layered textures, luxurious materials and settings, a sense of display.

  2. Remotetointimate

    Remote. Distant or withheld; the person is far off, closed, aloof or monumental, or there is no one to be close to.

    Intimate. Close and personal; the viewer feels near the person, in a private, unguarded moment.

  3. Naturalistictostaged

    Naturalistic. Could be a real moment in a real place; natural light, an unforced posture, nothing visibly built.

    Staged. Visibly constructed; a built set, a posed tableau, theatrical or artificial light.

  4. Classicaltocontemporary

    Classical. Timeless codes; heritage, elegance and craft that could belong to any decade.

    Contemporary. Of this moment; current youth culture, technology, street references, the look of now.

  5. Restrainedtoprovocative

    Restrained. Discreet and composed; nothing that confronts, unsettles or flirts.

    Provocative. Made to provoke a reaction; confronting, overtly sensual, strange or unsettling.

Each picture is placed by comparing it with six others, both ways round, so the order they are shown in cannot decide.

Colour and light

Measured from the pixels, with no reader involved.

  • LightnessHow bright the picture is overall.
  • ContrastHow far its brightness spreads from dark to light.
  • High key, low keyHow much of it is near white, and how much near black.
  • ChromaHow strong its colour is on average, and how much of it is in strong colour.
  • WarmthReds, oranges and yellows against greens and blues.
  • Spread of huesOne hue throughout, or many.
  • ColourfulnessA standard measure of how colourful a picture looks (Hasler and Süsstrunk).

How it works

How each description is checked, and how the stages, attention and movement are judged.

The checks

  • Checked by peopleA description stands only where people reading the same pictures, one of them trained in fashion, agree with it beyond chance. Until they have checked it, it is marked provisional.
  • BlindThe readers are never told the brand, the date or where a picture ran.
  • RepeatableEach reader is held to one fixed version, so any reading can be run again.
  • Against controlsEvery measure is also run on brands and dates where nothing happened, to see how often it fires anyway.
  • Proven firstEach measure is run on simulated brands with known answers before it touches real ones.

Before a reader is used it is tested on 296 pictures from 23 brands: whether its answers vary from picture to picture, whether two crops of one picture get the same answer, whether it tells brands apart, and how often it picks the picture a person picked across 150 pairs judged by hand.

Each stage, end to end

Each brand's collection is followed from its show to its advertising, and every measure is set against the other twenty-seven brands in the same season, like for like.

  1. The six months beforeThe sceneThe ambassadors the brand appointed, kept as context.
  2. The days around itThe showUp to twenty looks from the brand's own runway pages, translated into numbers; how far attention jumped against the other shows of its season; and how the press wrote about it.
  3. The six months afterThe shop windowWhat the brand put on its homepage.
  4. The seasonThe campaignsWhat it published for the season, and who shot it.
  5. Around the show, then to the next oneThe advertisingIts paid ads in the weeks around the show, then the campaign's until a week before its next show.

The clothes are described in the same terms at every stage, the runway included, and the pictures also for how they are made; Readings shows both. Attention is the jump at the show against the rest of its season, and whether it lasts, beside how much the press wrote and in what tone; the sections below set out how each is judged. The question is which of the season benchmark's four measures go with attention that lasts.

The monthly reading

Is the image drifting?

The brand's image read month by month, against its own past and its peers, each reading with its uncertainty.

  • CharacterIts imagery on eighteen questions, on the axes of luxury that pass the tests and in colour and light, with the cast of people who make it.
  • ShiftHow far the image has moved from its own past and against its peers, and whether it is drifting towards the brand of the moment.
  • ContributionThe attention that follows, and in time the desire and revenue, beyond what the brand's own momentum and the reach it paid for would deliver. A brand can set it against its own sales.
  • AlignmentIts advertising set against its show notes and campaign statements, question by question.

For a brand's president or image director, the group that owns it, or an investor following it.

How the stages are joined
The scene
Ambassador appointments in the 180 days before the show.
The show
The day before to three days after.
The shop window
The homepage from the day after the show to six months on.
The campaigns
By the season a campaign's title names, or else the brand's last main show within six months.
The advertising
Ads first seen from a week before the show to three weeks after, and the campaign's from six weeks after to a week before the brand's next main show. Only ads for the season's collection count towards the campaign, sorted by their own words; leather goods, beauty, jewellery and watches, capsules and the holidays are read apart. The campaign has landed when its third ad for the collection is first seen.
Each against its kind
A runway is set against the season's other runways and an advertisement against other advertisements. The show and the advertising do different jobs; what they share is the clothes.
How attention is judged
The jump
The peak of daily views from the day before a show to three days after, over the brand's level from 60 to 10 days before, set against the other shows of its season.
Lasting
The level from 30 to 120 days after, over the same baseline, less the median brand's change over the same days.
The campaign's lift
The level from the day the campaign lands to 60 days on, over the 60 to 10 days before it, less the median brand's change. Campaigns mostly arrive as the lasting window closes, so the advertising is read on its own clock.
The scene
New ambassadors in the 180 days before a show, held constant where the appointments are on file, from 2019, so a new face in the front row is not read as the clothes.
Sticky
Whether a show's attention lasts better than an equal jump on an ordinary day, taken at dates at least 30 days from any show of the brand.
Momentum
The trend the brand brought into the show, held constant, so a brand already rising does not read as a show that worked.
Press
The volume and tone of news about the brand on the show day and the three days after, against its usual level.
How movement is judged
Once
A picture counts once, in the month it was first shown, however long it stays up.
Like for like
Campaign pictures against campaign pictures, product on a model against product on a model, so showing more product is not read as a change of style.
Net of the market
Each brand's move less the same kind of move among its peers over the same months.
Against chance
Whole months are moved between the two periods being compared, since one month's homepage is often one campaign in several crops; and every result allows for how many brands were looked at.
Turnover or drift
A brand's pictures against its own from one, two, three and more half-years earlier, beside another brand's.
New designers
The year before a creative director's first show against the year that starts three months after it, beside brands with no change over the same months.
Success
Each brand against its own usual and net of the market, with the momentum it already had held constant.

Who makes the image

A brand's image is made by a cast: the designer, and the photographers, stylists and directors around them. Focal reads the cast as part of the brand's character: how long a brand keeps the same image-makers, how much of its image comes from one or two hands, how many of them also work for its peers, and who arrives and leaves with a designer.
