Does a new creative director change how a house advertises?

The question

Between July 2025 and February 2026, ten houses showed a new creative director's first collection. Adtone asks whether their advertising changed more than that of eight houses whose leadership held, using the ads Meta keeps for a year under the Digital Services Act.

How it measures

Each house is measured against the controls month by month, so a movement of the whole market doesn't read as a movement of one house. Three tests were frozen on 5 October 2026, before a single ad was collected.

  • Did the ten move further after their debuts than the controls moved at placebo dates?
  • Did Gucci's advertising move towards Balenciaga's, following Demna from one house to the other?
  • Does a scan that is not told the dates find the breaks in the ten, and stay quiet on the controls?

It reads paid advertising on Meta's platforms in Europe and Britain, not print, posters or the clothes themselves. It can show that a house's advertising changed, not that the change worked.

A prediction, made in advance

Frozen before Saint Laurent named a successor to Anthony Vaccarello: after the successor's first show, its advertising should shift more than nine in ten control placebos, break within 120 days, and move towards the successor's former house if that house is collected. Judged once, 270 days after the show.

Change and attention

The obvious heat measure, the Lyst Index, changed its method in early 2026 and began counting Chanel and Dior, so its rankings either side of the debuts don't compare. Wikipedia page views, counted the same way throughout, stand in as attention: whether bigger shifts drew more of it, not whether they sold more.
