End to end

Each brand's collection is followed from its show to its advertising, and every measure is set against the other twenty-seven brands in the same season, like for like.

  1. The six months beforeThe sceneThe ambassadors the brand appointed, kept as context.
  2. The days around itThe showAbout twenty looks from the brand's own runway pages, translated into numbers; how far attention jumped against the other shows of its season; and how the press wrote about it.
  3. The six months afterThe shop windowWhat the brand put on its homepage.
  4. The seasonThe campaignsWhat it published for the season, and who shot it.
  5. Around the show, then to the next oneThe advertisingIts paid ads in the weeks around the show, then the campaign's until a week before its next show.

Two descriptions

The clothes are described in the same terms at every stage, the runway included: garments, silhouette, colour, pattern, materials and formality. The pictures the brand makes after the show are also read for how they are made: picture kind, light, setting, people and pose, framing and mood, with colour and light measured from the pixels and a numerical fingerprint of each picture.

Four measures

  • TransmissionHow much of the runway's clothes reaches the shop window, the campaigns and the advertising, and how fast.
  • DistinctnessHow far the brand stands from its peers at each stage, so a distinctive runway flattened into look-alike advertising shows up as exactly that.
  • MixHow much of each stage is campaign imagery, product on a model and product alone.
  • MovementHow far each stage has moved from the brand's own previous seasons.

Against attention

The jump in attention at the show against the rest of its season, whether it lasts from 30 to 120 days after, and how much the press wrote and in what tone. The question is which of the four measures go with attention that lasts, brand by brand and across all twenty-eight.

548 collections followed so far: the scene covered for 330, the show scored for 501 and whether its attention lasted for 484, the shop window read for 175, the campaigns joined for 81, and the advertising for none until Meta's archive opens. The runway looks are being collected, and the description of the clothes is still being tested.

What it shows

Four readings for every brand, every month.

Character

What is the brand's image?

Eighteen measured qualities of its imagery, from light and setting to mood; where it sits on five axes of luxury; its colour and light; and the cast of people who make it.

Shift

How is it changing?

How far the image has moved from its own past and against its peers, how consistent it stays, and whether it is drifting towards the brand of the moment.

Contribution

What does it add to success?

The attention, desire and revenue that follow, beyond what the brand's own momentum and the reach it paid for would deliver, down to whether a show's attention lasts and the campaign carries it.

Alignment

Does the image say what the brand says?

Its advertising set against its show notes and campaign statements, question by question, beside the same check for its peers.

Each image is read against eighteen fixed questions, placed on five axes by comparing it with other luxury pictures, measured for colour and light, and kept as its answers, its places and a numerical fingerprint. Every brand is measured against its peers month by month, so a move by the whole market doesn't read as a move by one brand.

Contribution firms up as the record grows: each month adds attention, each quarter adds desire and revenue.

Read as luxury

Most luxury pictures share a register: polished, posed and quiet. Questions asked of one picture at a time say little about what sets one apart from another, so each picture is also read against others like it, on five axes.

  1. Austeretoopulent

    Austere. Spare and reduced; few elements, plain surfaces, a quiet palette, nothing decorative.

    Opulent. Rich and abundant; ornament, layered textures, luxurious materials and settings, a sense of display.

  2. Remotetointimate

    Remote. Distant or withheld; the person is far off, closed, aloof or monumental, or there is no one to be close to.

    Intimate. Close and personal; the viewer feels near the person, in a private, unguarded moment.

  3. Naturalistictostaged

    Naturalistic. Could be a real moment in a real place; natural light, an unforced posture, nothing visibly built.

    Staged. Visibly constructed; a built set, a posed tableau, theatrical or artificial light.

  4. Classicaltocontemporary

    Classical. Timeless codes; heritage, elegance and craft that could belong to any decade.

    Contemporary. Of this moment; current youth culture, technology, street references, the look of now.

  5. Restrainedtoprovocative

    Restrained. Discreet and composed; nothing that confronts, unsettles or flirts.

    Provocative. Made to provoke a reaction; confronting, overtly sensual, strange or unsettling.

On each axis a picture is set beside six others and asked about both ways round, so the order they are shown in cannot decide. Its place on the axis comes from all its comparisons together, which places pictures within a brand as well as between brands.

Colour and light

Measured from the pixels, with no reader involved.

  • LightnessHow bright the picture is overall.
  • ContrastHow far its brightness spreads from dark to light.
  • High key, low keyHow much of it is near white, and how much near black.
  • ChromaHow strong its colour is on average, and how much of it is in strong colour.
  • WarmthReds, oranges and yellows against greens and blues.
  • Spread of huesOne hue throughout, or many.
  • ColourfulnessA standard measure of how colourful a picture looks (Hasler and Süsstrunk).

How it works

The method in outline, from the runway to the attention that follows.

The runway

About twenty looks from each show, taken from the brand's own runway pages as public web archives keep them, each described for its clothes: garments, silhouette, colour, pattern, materials and formality.

The pictures

What the brand puts out after the show, on its homepage, in its campaigns and in its paid advertising, described for the same clothes and for how each picture is made, with colour and light measured from the pixels.

Attention

Daily Wikipedia page views for every brand, in ten languages: the jump at each show against the rest of its season, and whether it lasts. Beside them, how much the press wrote and in what tone.

The links

Every stage on windows counted from the show date, the clothes described in the same terms throughout, and every measure set against the other twenty-seven brands in the same season, like for like.

The checks

  • Checked by peopleA description is used only where people reading the same pictures, one of them trained in fashion, agree with it beyond chance.
  • BlindThe reader sees each picture alone, never the brand, the date or where it ran.
  • RepeatableEach reader is held to one version, so any reading can be run again and give the same answer.
  • Against controlsEvery measure is also run on brands and dates where nothing happened, to see how often it fires anyway.
  • Proven firstEach measure is run on simulated brands with known answers before it touches real ones.

Before a reader is used it is tested on 296 pictures from 23 brands: whether its answers vary from picture to picture, whether two crops of one picture get the same answer, whether it tells brands apart, and how often it picks the picture a person picked across 150 pairs judged by hand.

How the stages are joined
The scene
Ambassador appointments in the 180 days before the show.
The show
The day before to three days after.
The shop window
The homepage from the day after the show to six months on.
The campaigns
By the season a campaign's title names, or else the brand's last main show within six months.
The advertising
Ads first seen from a week before the show to three weeks after, and the campaign's from six weeks after to a week before the brand's next main show.
How attention is judged
The jump
The peak of daily views from the day before a show to three days after, over the brand's level from 60 to 10 days before, set against the other shows of its season.
Lasting
The level from 30 to 120 days after, over the same baseline, less the median brand's change over the same days.
Sticky
Whether a show's attention lasts better than an equal jump on an ordinary day, taken at dates at least 30 days from any show of the brand.
Momentum
The trend the brand brought into the show, held constant, so a brand already rising does not read as a show that worked.
Press
The volume and tone of news about the brand on the show day and the three days after, against its usual level.
How movement is judged
Once
A picture counts once, in the month it was first shown, however long it stays up.
Like for like
Campaign pictures against campaign pictures, product on a model against product on a model, so showing more product is not read as a change of style.
Net of the market
Each brand's move less the same kind of move among its peers over the same months.
Against chance
Whole months are moved between the two periods being compared, since one month's homepage is often one campaign in several crops; and every result allows for how many brands were looked at.
Turnover or drift
A brand's pictures against its own from one, two, three and more half-years earlier, beside another brand's.
New designers
The year before a creative director's first show against the year that starts three months after it, beside brands with no change over the same months.
Success
Each brand against its own usual and net of the market, with the momentum it already had held constant.

Who makes the image

A brand's image is made by a cast: the designer, and the photographers, stylists and directors around them. Focal reads the cast as part of the brand's character.

Continuity

How long a brand keeps the same image-makers.

Concentration

How much of its image comes from one or two hands.

Overlap

How many of its image-makers also work for its peers, which pulls looks together.

Movement

Who arrives and leaves with a designer, and what changes with them.

Shared hands

Some photographers shape several brands at once. David Sims has shot for nine of the twenty-eight, Steven Meisel for seven.
