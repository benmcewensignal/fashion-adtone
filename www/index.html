Luxury advertising runs on hope

Houses count every impression, every euro of media value and every sale. The look itself, what the advertising actually shows, goes unmeasured. Adtone measures it as the ads run: whether a house's advertising says what the house says, whether a new creative director's look has reached the campaigns, and whose look it is when designers and their image-makers move between houses.

Underneath is a research question, with its tests frozen before any data: whose look is it, and does it move people?

How it's tested

Under the Digital Services Act, Meta keeps every ad shown in the European Union for a year after it last ran. Each image is read against eighteen fixed questions, from light and setting to mood, and kept only as its answers and a numerical fingerprint. Each house is measured against the controls month by month, so a movement of the whole market doesn't read as a movement of one house.

Three tests were frozen on 5 October 2026, before a single ad was collected.

  • Shift. Did the ten move further after their debuts than the controls moved at placebo dates?
  • Transfer. Did Gucci's advertising move towards Balenciaga's earlier look, following Demna? This is the mechanism: a look that belongs to people should travel with them.
  • Detection. Does a scan that isn't told the dates find the breaks, and stay quiet on the controls?

A prediction, made in advance

Frozen before Saint Laurent named a successor to Anthony Vaccarello: after the successor's first show, its advertising should shift more than nine in ten control placebos, break within 120 days, and move towards the successor's former house if that house is collected. Judged once, 270 days after the show.

What it can't say

It reads paid advertising on Meta's platforms in Europe and Britain, not print or the clothes. Image-makers moved with the designers, so a transfer is read as a team's, not a designer's alone. And it can show that advertising changed, not that the change worked.

What the design can detect

A simulation run before any data, on the real calendar, found that collecting from early October leaves about six of the ten houses testable. Starting in December leaves about one. So results will lead with the pooled shift, the transfer family and how often the controls raise a false alarm, never with a ranking of houses.

From reading to result

A reading is worth what it predicts. Each month, every house gets four readings of its advertising: how far its look moved from its own past, how far it sits from the market, how consistent it is, and whether it is drifting towards the house of the moment.

Revenue at the seven houses that report it by house.

Quarterly

The test is whether this month's readings predict next month's change, beyond each house's own momentum, the reach it paid for and the months after a debut. It counts only months after the test is frozen and after six months of live collection; everything earlier trains the model and never scores it. If the look predicts curiosity but not desire, it got people talking without creating demand.

Lyst changed its method in early 2026 and began counting Chanel and Dior, so its rankings are compared only within the new method. This test is planned for the second amendment, to be frozen before any data is collected. There are no results yet.

Before the forecast

Whether the look moves people is the forecast, and it needs about eighteen months of live data to prove itself. The alignment check and the insights don't wait for it.

Alignment check

Does the advertising say what the house says?

Show notes and campaign statements are read into the same eighteen questions as the ads. The check reports, question by question, where the advertising carries the stated intent and where it parts from it, set beside the same check for the house's peers.
