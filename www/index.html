Does a new creative director change how a house advertises?

The question

Between July 2025 and February 2026, ten houses showed a new creative director's first collection. Adtone asks whether their advertising changed more than that of eight houses whose leadership held.

Under the Digital Services Act, Meta keeps every ad shown in the European Union for a year after it last ran, and British ads are kept as well. That record is the material.

How it measures

Each image is scored against a rubric frozen by its hash and placed by a fixed model. Each house is measured against the controls month by month, so a movement of the whole market does not read as a movement of one house.

Three tests were written and frozen on 5 October 2026, before a single ad was collected.

  • Did the ten move further after their debuts than the controls moved at placebo dates?
  • Did Gucci's advertising move towards Balenciaga's, following Demna from one house to the other?
  • Does a scan that is not told the dates find the breaks in the ten, and stay quiet on the controls?

A prediction, made in advance

When it was frozen on 5 October 2026, Anthony Vaccarello's departure from Saint Laurent had been reported but not confirmed, and no successor had been named.

After the successor's first show, Saint Laurent's advertising should shift more than nine in ten control placebos, with the break inside 120 days, and towards the successor's former house if that house is collected. It is judged once, 270 days after the show, and void if there is no show by the end of 2027.

Change and attention

The obvious measure of brand heat, the Lyst Index, changed its method in early 2026 and began counting Chanel and Dior that quarter, so its rankings either side of the debuts do not compare.

Attention is measured instead from Wikipedia page views, counted the same way throughout, and set against each house's shift. It describes whether bigger shifts drew more attention. It cannot say whether they sold more.

What it cannot say

It reads paid advertising on Meta's platforms in Europe and Britain. Not print, posters, runways or the clothes themselves.

It can show that a house's advertising changed. It cannot show that the change worked.
